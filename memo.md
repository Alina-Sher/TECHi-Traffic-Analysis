# TECHi Traffic Assessment

## Executive conclusion

The claim that TECHi traffic is “up 30% YoY” cannot be verified from the supplied data because there is no September 2024–August 2025 traffic period for a true year-over-year comparison.

The supplied period does show reported sessions increasing from 57,500 in September 2025 to 102,805 in August 2026, an increase of 78.8%, but this is not a YoY result. More importantly, the February 2026 analytics change creates a measurement discontinuity, so the raw increase should not be interpreted as equivalent to real audience growth.

## A. Is traffic really up 30% YoY?

Not verifiable from the supplied data. A true YoY calculation requires the same months from the previous year.

The strongest visible discontinuity is January to February 2026: reported sessions increased from 64,000 to 94,685, or 48.0%. The increase occurred across all six sections, which is consistent with the site-wide analytics snippet change on 1 February rather than an abrupt increase in readers.

## B. Best estimate of underlying change

I used the five non-Guides sections to estimate the measurement change. Their combined sessions increased from 60,300 in January to 89,465 in February, implying a measurement factor of approximately 1.4837.

Applying this factor to August's reported 102,805 sessions gives an estimated 69,291 sessions on the pre-February measurement basis. Compared with January's 64,000 sessions, this suggests underlying growth of approximately 8.3%.

This should be treated as an estimate, not a precise reconstruction, because the analytics implementation details and user-level data are not provided.

The November Crypto spike is also important. Crypto sessions increased from 10,100 in October to 24,800 in November (+145.5%), then fell to 11,000 in December. This pattern is consistent with a one-off aggregator exposure rather than sustained organic growth.

## C. Should Guides be retired?

Yes — I would retire Guides as a standalone publishing priority and redirect limited editorial effort elsewhere.

Guides traffic fell from 3,700 sessions in January 2026 to 725 in August, a decline of 80.4%. It had already fallen from 2,175 in April to 725 in August. The assessment brief also states that ads pay per session and that the value of a session on Guides is near zero.

The recommendation has a caveat: no revenue data is supplied, so the exact financial cost or benefit of retiring Guides cannot be quantified. A safer implementation would be to preserve useful evergreen Guides rather than delete them immediately.

## Two numeric findings from Part 1

- Markets: 16 of 20 sampled articles (80%) name a company.
- Markets: 14 of 20 sampled articles (70%) contain a number in the headline.

These findings show that the current Markets sample is relatively company- and number-focused, but the Part 1 snapshot does not prove that these characteristics caused traffic performance.

## Where Part 1 and the traffic data meet

Part 1 provides a current snapshot of the newest articles, while the traffic table provides monthly section traffic. The datasets can therefore be compared for context, but they cannot establish historical causality because Part 1 does not contain monthly publishing volumes.

## Recommendation and cost if wrong

I recommend retiring Guides as a standalone publishing priority while preserving strong evergreen pages. The main risk is losing the remaining 725 monthly sessions and any long-tail search or newsletter value generated by those pages. This risk can be reduced by keeping high-performing evergreen Guides and redirecting new editorial effort toward higher-value sections.

I would change my mind if... a validated analytics export showed that the February implementation change did not alter session counting, or if matched pre/post implementation data demonstrated a genuine approximately 30% increase in unique users.

## Next number I would want, free to get

I would request monthly unique users by section for the months before and after 1 February 2026, together with documentation of exactly how the analytics implementation changed. Unique users would help distinguish more people visiting TECHi from the same people generating more measured sessions.
